### SECTION 3 — M3 STICKY CASE PANEL

**About.** Powerlook is a premium men’s fashion brand in India. It came to Growisto with a platform problem, not a growth problem: the store it already had could not stay up.

**Services.** Website & Tech · CRO

**Period.** 3-month migration

### SECTION 4 — M4 THEIR STORY

Eyebrow: Their story

## A growing brand held back by the platform underneath it

Powerlook had demand. What it did not have was a storefront that could absorb it. The Magento and React stack crashed under load, and every hour of downtime came straight off revenue.

At roughly $105K a month in lost sales, the cost of staying put had already passed the cost of moving. The risk was that a migration done badly would cost even more.

### SECTION 5 — M5 THE CHALLENGE

Eyebrow: The challenge

## Replace the platform without losing a day of trading

Rescue a failing platform bleeding roughly $105K monthly, evaluate the best platform options through rigorous analysis, and execute a complete migration within three months with zero downtime.

Constraint tiles: Crashes under load · Revenue lost monthly · Festive peak approaching

**Objectives.** Choose the platform on evidence rather than preference, align every team on that choice before a line of code was written, and treat the migration as a conversion opportunity rather than a like-for-like port.

### SECTION 6 — M6 THE SOLUTION

Eyebrow: The solution

## Evidence first, alignment second, migration third

Three moves carried the rebuild: a platform decision made on measured criteria, a planning phase that removed disagreement before the build, and a migration that carried a CRO roadmap with it.

#### 01 The call — The platform was chosen on measured criteria, not preference

Three candidates were compared on performance, scalability, and total cost of ownership.

Slot type: **comparison table**

| Option | Assessment | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Magento Headless | Familiar stack, unchanged cost profile | Not selected |
| Shopify Plus Headless | Strong performance, highest build cost | Not selected |
| Shopify Plus | Best balance of performance, scale and TCO | Selected |

The comparison ran across performance, scalability, and total cost of ownership. Shopify Plus won on the combination, not on any single axis.

#### 02 The build — Planned until there was nothing left to argue about

Slot type: **phase row**

| Phase | Title | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Platform evaluation | Compare the three candidates on measured criteria | Performance, scalability, TCO |
| Phase 2 | Alignment | Multiple sessions with tech, marketing and management | One agreed scope |
| Phase 3 | Migration with CRO | Rebuild against a conversion roadmap, not a like-for-like port | — |
| Phase 4 | Launch | Full Shopify Plus platform live in three months | Zero downtime, zero bugs |

Disagreement was resolved before the build, not during it.

#### 03 The real test — The festive season, on a platform three months old

A migration is only proven under load. Powerlook’s first festive season on Shopify Plus ran 80% ahead of the same period on the old platform, which is the number that matters: the peak that used to take the store down became the peak it grew through.

Slot type: **time series**

Axis: Pre-migration → Launch → Festive season

Caption: Illustrative shape only — real revenue export pending

Eyebrow: The outcome

Avaana helps Australian businesses through NDIS provider registration and growth. Almost all of its search visibility was branded, which meant it could only be found by people who already knew the name. The work was to build demand from people who did not.

### SECTION 3 — M3 STICKY CASE PANEL

**About.** Avaana is an Australian consultancy that helps businesses through NDIS provider registration and growth, covering application preparation, audit coaching, and tailored business strategy.

**Services.** GEO, AIO & SEO

**Period.** 12 months

### SECTION 4 — M4 THEIR STORY

Eyebrow: Their story

## A consultancy that could only be found by people who knew its name

Avaana works at the regulated end of Australian healthcare: NDIS provider registration, audit coaching, and the business strategy that follows. Its buyers are operators with a specific compliance problem and a deadline.

Those buyers were searching. They were not searching for Avaana, because they had no reason to know it existed. Revenue leaned on paid channels because organic was carrying almost nothing that was not branded.

### SECTION 5 — M5 THE CHALLENGE

Eyebrow: The challenge

## All the visibility was branded, and branded does not scale

Increase organic traffic through on-page, off-page and technical SEO, focused on how operators actually search for NDIS regulation, registration, and provider services.

Constraint tiles: 16 non-brand clicks · Paid-dependent revenue · No AI presence

**Objectives.** Build non-brand demand around regulation, registration and services intent, and be present when those same questions are asked of an AI assistant rather than typed into a search box.

### SECTION 6 — M6 THE SOLUTION

Eyebrow: The solution

## Map the demand, earn the pages, then be citable

Three moves carried the engagement: a content map built from real keyword demand, a technical and on-page program against the pages that could rank, and content shaped to be quoted by LLMs as well as ranked by Google.

#### 01 The map — 900+ keywords analyzed, then narrowed to what could actually rank

Coverage was decided by demand and winnability, not by volume alone.

Slot type: **funnel**

| Value | Stage |
|---|---|
| **900+** | High-relevance keywords analyzed |
| **62** | Unique blog pages mapped |
| **17** | Priority pages optimized |

Caption: Each stage narrows to what the site could realistically own, rather than publishing against every term.

The narrowing is the work. Mapping 900 keywords to 62 pages, and then concentrating on 17, is what made a small site competitive on regulated-sector terms.

#### 02 The build — On-page, technical and authority, run together

Slot type: **phase row**

| Phase | Title | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Content strategy | 900+ keywords analyzed and mapped to intent | Regulation, registration, services |
| Phase 2 | On-page optimization | 17 priority pages rewritten and restructured | — |
| Phase 3 | Technical audit | Page speed, coverage issues and site parameters | — |
| Phase 4 | Authority | Targeted backlinking against the priority pages | — |

Non-brand clicks moved from 16 a month to 516 over the twelve months.

#### 03 The real test — Being found by an AI assistant, not just by Google

Content was optimized to surface inside AI and LLM results, not only in Google’s organic listings. Over 100 sessions arrived from AI assistants, contributing a 20% lift to organic traffic. For a regulated category where buyers ask an assistant what the rules are before they ask who can help, that channel is the leading indicator.

Slot type: **split bar**

| Segment | Share |
|---|---|
| Conventional organic | 80% |
| LLM-sourced | 20% |

Caption: Share of organic traffic contributed by sessions originating in AI assistants

# How Nobero Grew Sales From Ads 378% While Lifting ROAS 26.5%

A homegrown fashion athleisure brand with a seasonal pull toward winter, competing at a premium price in one of the most contested categories on Amazon. Scaling spend was the easy part. Scaling it without losing efficiency was the account.

Byline: Written by Growisto · [Publish date pending]

### SECTION 2 — M2 RESULTS

## Results

Period label: Full-year 2024

| Stat | Label | Explainer |
|---|---|---|
| **378%** | Increase in sales from ads | Across 2024 |
| **26.5%** | Increase in ROAS | Achieved while spend was being scaled heavily, not held flat |
| **65%** | Increase in conversion rate | More of the traffic the account bought went on to convert |
| **278%** | Increase in ad spend | The input behind the growth. Sales outgrew spend, which is where the ROAS lift comes from |

Link: How these numbers were measured

### SECTION 3 — M3 STICKY CASE PANEL

**About.** Nobero is a homegrown fashion athleisure brand in India, with a seasonal influence through the winter season and a premium price point in a highly competitive category.

**Services.** Marketplaces · Amazon Ads

**Period.** 2024

### SECTION 4 — M4 THEIR STORY

Eyebrow: Their story

## A seasonal brand competing at a premium in fashion

Nobero sells athleisure into a category where competition is heavy and price pressure is constant. Its assortment leans toward winter, which concentrates a large share of the year into a short window.

The brand needed to scale, not just spend. Buying more traffic is trivial in fashion; buying it at a price that still returns, through a seasonal peak, against better-known names, is the actual problem.

### SECTION 5 — M5 THE CHALLENGE

Eyebrow: The challenge

## Scale the account without buying unprofitable growth

Four objectives ran in parallel: scale account sales sustainably, optimize advertising spend while improving conversion rate, identify new targeting areas to lift brand visibility and sales, and build brand salience in a highly competitive fashion category.

Constraint tiles: Seasonal assortment · Premium price point · Highly competitive category

**Objectives.** Grow sales faster than spend, so that scale and efficiency move together rather than trading off against each other.

### SECTION 6 — M6 THE SOLUTION

Eyebrow: The solution

## A hero product, an early scale-up, and automation holding the budget

Three moves carried the year: concentrate on a premium niche and a hero product, scale early to catch the season rather than chase it, and let automation manage budget in real time so efficiency held while spend climbed.

#### 01 The call — Scale early to catch the season, rather than bid into it

A winter-weighted assortment decides its year in a short window. Position has to exist before the window opens.

Slot type: **comparison table**

| Approach | When spend lands | Position entering peak |
|---|---|---|
| Scale into the peak | With every competitor at once | Starting from scratch, at peak CPC |
| Scale up early | Ahead of the seasonal curve | Already ranked, at lower cost |

Paired with a deliberate narrowing: focus on a premium niche, and build a hero product rather than spreading budget evenly across the catalog.

#### 02 The build — Find the opportunity, then let rules hold the line

Slot type: **phase row**

| Phase | Title | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step 1 | Hero product focus | Concentrate on a premium niche and build the hero SKU | Premium niche |
| Step 2 | Early seasonal scale-up | Capitalize on seasonal growth before the peak | — |
| Step 3 | Keyword opportunity mapping | Identify keyword opportunities across the category | — |
| Step 4 | Rule-based optimization | Automated rules applied to lift account ROAS | — |

Real-time budget management through our automation layer prevented sales loss during the scale-up.

#### 03 The real test — Sales outgrew spend, which is the whole point

Ad spend rose 278% across 2024. Sales from ads rose 378%. Because sales outgrew spend, ROAS went up 26.5% in the same period, during the heaviest scaling the account had seen. The gap between those two lines is the result; the headline number on its own would not show it.

Slot type: **bar comparison**

| Series | Value |
|---|---|
| Sales from ads | **378%** |
| Ad spend | **278%** |
| ROAS | **26.5%** |

Caption: Sales from ads grew 4.78x while spend grew 3.78x. The ratio between them is the 26.5% ROAS lift.

### SECTION 7 — M7 THE OUTCOME

Eyebrow: The outcome

## What changed beyond the ad line

Headline stat: **65%**

Increase in conversion rate, so the traffic the account was buying converted at a materially better rate by the end of the year.

Source line: [Measurement window and comparison baseline pending]

Second outcome: The brand reports a hero SKU, the travel hoodie, carrying roughly 30% of sales, with several SKUs reaching top-ranked positions in the category.

### SECTION 8 — M8 VOICE

> “The last two years journey with Growisto on Amazon has been a remarkable story. We being a niche business in the athleisure space, and the business was also seasonal which had hoodies as a large part of the assortment, and the price point was also premium. So how do we win against brands which are lower competition and how we build visibility was the problem statement. Growisto suggested we build visibility ahead of the season and this really worked out well for us.”
